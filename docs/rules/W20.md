# W20: Low text contrast on an underlying filled shape

| | |
|---|---|
| Severity | warning |
| Category | render |
| Profiles | full |

## What it means

Text drawn over a filled shape that sits below it in z-order, at a contrast below 2.0:1 against that shape -- a caption laid across a chart's bars, a footnote dropped onto a colored panel, a label that slid onto a card during an edit round. W19 answers the same question one shape earlier and stops at the run's own fill; W15 needs two texts colliding, and a bar holds no text of its own, so this case falls between them. The same verdict extends one layer further down to the page itself: a transparent text frame is judged against the resolved slide background (an explicit solid p:bg at slide, layout, or master level, the stock bgRef-1001 style through the theme, or a full-bleed solid rectangle painted as the ground), which is how ghost text left on an empty slide finally gets caught. The verdict also reaches inside vector pictures: a chart carried as an svgBlip keeps its strings and fills as XML, so a caption buried on a shape within the SVG is judged the same way (a PNG chart erases its text and stays out of reach -- preserving text is the exporter's side of the contract). Backgrounds and underlying fills that cannot be decoded from XML -- gradients, picture fills, url() paints, nonstandard background style references -- abstain as w20_fill_unknown rather than guess. Coverage is summed across every low-contrast shape under the run, because a caption spanning two bars is buried by both. Uses the same 2.0:1 line as W19 and a 50% coverage floor that still needs a corpus sweep, so it runs in the full profile only while that soaks.

## How to fix

Move the text off the shape, or change either color to reach at least 2:1.
